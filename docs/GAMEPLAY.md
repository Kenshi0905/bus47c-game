# Gameplay and implementation notes

> Contains full story spoilers. For downloads and a spoiler-light introduction, return to the [project page](../README.md).

A short first-person low-poly horror game about a firewatcher's ride home to Loomesville.

## Play beta 0.10.0

For the standalone Windows beta, extract the complete ZIP into a folder and run **Bus47C.exe** beside its **Bus47C.pck**. Read the included beta notes. More lore is planned; this is not the final release.

On this development PC, double-click **Start Game.cmd**. To edit, double-click **Open Editor.cmd**. On a fresh Windows checkout, run `powershell -ExecutionPolicy Bypass -File tools/setup.ps1` first to download the pinned official Godot runtime and import assets. The runtime is intentionally not committed.

WASD moves, mouse looks, hold right-click to zoom, E inspects/interacts/sits, Space stands, Tab opens shift notes, Shift walks faster and Escape pauses. Read the bench note, board through the open doors, and inspect the empty driver's seat and dashboard clock. All intermediate stops depart automatically after boarding; the first passenger has no look-back prompt or required interaction. Start a **New Journey** to experience the whole expanded route; older checkpoints still load.

Six stops including the starting shelter and terminus: **Firewatch Shelter → Mill Road → Old Quarry → Pine Crossing → St Bride's → Loomesville**. Five passengers board along the route and leave at randomly assigned later stops. A saved journey keeps the same departure schedule. Each departing passenger pauses to stare at you, walks into the shelter and gradually sheds its eyes, nose and mouth before becoming faceless. On the next leg, look through the side windows for a matching headless body in a pool of blood. Its initial reveal follows the side you are viewing, appears through fog at a distance and stays fixed afterward. A moving deer crosses during St Bride's to Loomesville; the bus breaks down about 220 metres before the terminus. Journey legs are roughly 25 to 31 percent longer than version 0.7. The repaired final ride lasts 46 seconds and rounds several bends. The continuation opens automatically after the impact, even if you miss the deer interaction.

The title menu slowly moves its camera through the live scene. Pause offers Main Menu and Exit to Windows; returning to the title preserves the last checkpoint. Settings include volume, sensitivity, field of view, subtitles and an optional retro picture filter. Pixel sampling, restrained palette dithering, grain, scanlines and vignette affect the world while text stays crisp. Menu design and asset editing notes are in [menu-design.txt](menu-design.txt).

The longer winding route passes through a dense forest, with darker moonlight and denser ground mist. Roadside stops have shelters, benches, timetables and lights. The watcher appears through the left or right window you are looking toward, with placement checks for window pillars. Ordinary gaze never dismisses him. Hold right-click and aim a settled zoom at him for about half a second: he turns over 1.9 seconds and stares. After the turn and stare, he glides rapidly to a real nearby tree, peeks for 2.8 seconds, retreats behind it and dissolves. Smoke and a red trace linger, with a positional rush and tail. An overlooked figure can relocate only after it is far behind and offscreen. No watcher encounter gates progression.

Doors open at each bus stop. Departure waits for passengers to board and leave, the player to return inside, a clear doorway and fully closed doors. You can step out and board again while stopped. Passengers enter out of view, walk along the aisle and sit when unobserved; their seats account for wherever you are sitting. The steering wheel responds to the road bends.

Thought subtitles are brief; sound descriptions can be enabled in Settings. Objectives appear in the top-left corner and remain available in the shift notes, rendered on fibrous paper with worn edges, stains and fold creases. Saves stay local and are excluded from Git; test runs use a separate save. A collision ramp under the visible steps supports physical boarding. The route is scripted, with multiple bends; the player is a passenger, not the driver.

After the breakdown, physically step off the bus. A stomach rumble plays once outside, followed by the persistent top-left objective "Find something to eat." Search nearby undergrowth and eat from three scattered fruit bushes; no marked trail leads directly to them. Hunger does not run down while waiting aboard. The hunger and footstep narration lines are removed. During the pursuit, a backward look reveals an empty forest. Staying still is safe; moving after that look triggers a close lunge and sound sting before failure. The fade clears to a visible retry menu; it cannot cover the buttons. Side glances do not arm it. A weathered, blood-streaked warning stands beside the path, with a smaller directional marker beneath it. Five distinct warnings, including the woods sign and an overhead gantry, replace repeated roadside pairs; their blood drops move while the game is running. Walking onto that path selects the route automatically and leads continuously through a gap in the village fence onto the street; pressing E at the sign is optional. After eating, you can take the footpath immediately or find five scattered engine parts. Collect each with E, then fit them at the front engine panel. The five-second repair pauses with the menu. Only a repaired bus can depart for the final stop. Collected parts and completed repairs survive retries.

Both story branches are playable prototypes. The shortcut leads to the hostile double, an E interaction that reveals the reflection, followed by three inspections that remove facial features. Leave bloody footprints as you physically walk Forestry Way, guided by a small arrow, then collapse, see your glowing red tie and wake at the morning shelter before the daylight ticket loop. The bus route leads to the faceless intruder, a defensive knife interaction, a three-minute body-dragging/burial task, 90 seconds hiding in the closet, dawn, coffee and the newspaper accusation. The knife is on a lit entry table, reachable from the doorway. Pick it up with E; the attacker starts advancing only after it is equipped. Left-click or E defends at close range, with a visible held knife and control hint. E also interacts with tools, body, grave and wardrobe. The body trails behind you while dragging. Pull its visible centre into the grave marker; it can be buried while held or after releasing it there. Opening the closet before daylight fails the sequence. Both endings offer a return to the fork.

A triangular deer-crossing warning beside the breakdown changes to a bloody crossed-out portrait only after you have seen it, looked away and collected supplies. Straying beyond the nearby search clearing triggers a backstab and first-person fall. Safe checkpoints cover the forest, fork, town encounter, burial, closet, Forestry Way departure and dawn. Pause and reading stop hunger, pursuit and burial/closet timers. Save data validates known checkpoints and continues to accept the earlier bus-only format.

Loomesville has houses, parked cars, lamps and overhead wires. Bus arrivals begin outside a welcome gate, with familiar shop-owner bodies and a corpse heap beyond it. No suited watcher appears in bus-route town. Both routes have bloodier shops. Only bus arrivals have the existing footprint trail; shortcut players start leaving their own prints after the mirror. The coffee counter lights go out once and stay out, including after reloading. Updated store signs and separated pavement edges complete this pass. Explore **Morrow's Takeaway**, **Night Owl Diner**, **47 Mart** and **Still Hour Coffee** through their open doors. Walk their aisles, inspect the furnishings and read optional counter notes with E. These four shops and house 17 have interiors; the other houses are exterior scenery. House 17 has a covered porch and a worn hall opening into side rooms. The mirror stays dark until E, the Forestry Way marker appears when it is relevant, and notes cross out completed tasks. Entering town disables the forest jumpscare even if you leave the marked path early to visit a shop.

Characters use editable mesh parts with procedural walking and separate seated poses. `town_district.gd` builds the town and house details, `horror_signs.gd` controls the weathered warning boards and falling blood, and `bus_passengers.gd` controls arrivals, departures and roadside bodies. Shop notes and names live in `town_district.gd`. Layouts, mesh parts, colors, shaders and timings remain editable. Story scenes remain in `aftermath.gd`; this is an initial gameplay/art pass, not a finished release. The mirror uses a staged portrait rather than a real-time room reflection; defense is an interaction rather than a combat system, and cries are synthesized rather than voiced dialogue. This milestone has Windows rendered and automated testing; Linux CI checks headless logic/physics. Browser, mobile, macOS and graphical Linux releases remain unverified.

## Beta audio and export

The golden path and Forestry Way use an edited Horror Bundle mood cue, with sparse original tension swells and pursuit footsteps that stop immediately when you stop. Separate edited cues mark the collapse, ambush and death. These four excerpts are included following the user's explicit free-use confirmation, recorded with source intervals in the audio-source notes. The watcher rush remains positional. Atmosphere ducks under events, menus pause ongoing tension, and death audio resolves over the visible retry screen. The supplied sound-design tutorial informed the arrangement.

For a reproducible Windows build, obtain the pinned export templates with `python tools/fetch_windows_template.py`, then run `powershell -ExecutionPolicy Bypass -File tools/export_beta.ps1`. The default output is ignored `work/beta-windows`; `-ZipPath` packages a chosen delivery path. The export includes engine, third-party and font notices, plus the audio permission record. The repository is public at the user's request.

## Presentation update in beta 0.10.0

The diner has red vinyl booths, cream tables, a lunch counter with stools, blinds and warm fluorescent lights. The title uses the approved F artwork. Share Tech Mono is the matching editable font across menus, notes and world labels.

Town bodies have generic E inspection prompts. The first inspection prompts a shocked reaction; later inspections describe small clues. No identity text is attached to a body. The hazard cross is made from uneven tapered strokes, smears and drips.

Inside the wardrobe, WASD shifts position within its bounds, mouse look remains available and held right-click opens a narrow peeking gap. A countdown shows the remaining time until daylight. E still leaves hiding and is fatal before the timer ends. Pausing or reading notes freezes the countdown.

Engine repair takes five seconds, burial eight and coffee preparation four. Each action displays its progress, current step and remaining time, and keeps movement locked until completion. Journal strike-through marks completed tasks; taking the footpath labels the unchosen repair as abandoned.

The newspaper is typeset into columns with game-rendered photographs and a matching physical prop. Closing the final document begins a 33-second first-person closing scene with a clock glance, a distant sound, reaction, fade and credits before results. Both endings remain pause-safe. The test suite covers these behaviors; native previews are in `docs/v0.10.0-beta.1-*.png`.

## Development

Godot 4.7.2 standard edition, GDScript, Compatibility renderer. Open `game/project.godot` in Godot. Original editable character sources are in `game/assets/characters`.

## Version history

Every finished development milestone is committed and pushed. Tested playable milestones receive annotated version tags. Use a separate checkout to inspect an old version without discarding current work: `git worktree add ../bus47c-v0.1.0 v0.1.0`.

Use `git revert <commit>` on a new branch to undo a change while preserving history. Never force-push the main branch. Run the smoke tests before tagging a playable build.

The surrounding folder contains unrelated media; it is deliberately excluded from this repository.
